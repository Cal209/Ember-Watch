The basics

How wildfires start, spread, and stop

A working knowledge of fire behavior is the difference between noticing something early and finding out too late.

01

Most are human-caused

Globally and in the U.S., the large majority of wildfires are started by people — downed power lines, unattended campfires, equipment sparks, arson, and discarded cigarettes — rather than lightning.

02

Wind drives the spread

Fire creates its own wind by pulling in air to feed combustion, and that inflow, combined with ambient wind, can push flame fronts forward far faster than a person can run uphill.

03

Fire can outrun you

Under strong wind and dry fuel, a grass fire can move faster than 6–7 mph — well beyond a brisk human jogging pace on uneven terrain.

04

Embers travel ahead of the fire

Burning embers can be lofted a mile or more downwind of the main fire, starting new "spot fires" well before the visible front arrives.

05

Dry fuel is the real accelerant

Drought-cured grass, dead brush, and low humidity matter more than temperature alone — they determine how readily a spark becomes a sustained fire.

06

Some ecosystems need fire

Many forests and grasslands evolved with periodic natural fire; certain seed cones only open under intense heat, and controlled burns are used deliberately to reduce future fuel loads.

07

Smoke is a health hazard far from the flames

Fine particulate smoke can travel hundreds of miles and worsen respiratory and cardiovascular conditions long after the fire itself has passed.

08

The first minutes matter most

A fire the size of a wastebasket can often be stopped with a bucket of water or an extinguisher — the same fire, ten minutes later, may need an entire crew.

Fire triangle

Remove one side, the fire stops

Every flame needs all three of the following at once. Firefighting is mostly about taking one of them away — that's also exactly what the response simulation asks you to do.

01

Fuel

Anything combustible — dry grass, brush, timber, structures.

02

Heat

An ignition source hot enough to start combustion and sustain it.

03

Oxygen

Ambient air feeding the chemical reaction — wind accelerates this.

How it works

  • Click any orange (burning) tile to extinguish it.
  • Each douse costs water; the tank refills automatically.
  • Every ~1.8s, burning tiles have a chance to ignite dry neighbors.
  • New, unrelated fires can also spark on their own every few seconds — stay alert to the whole grid, not just active fronts.
  • Green tiles are unburned fuel, black tiles are already burned out.
  • Survive 60 seconds with less than half the grid burned to win.

Why this mirrors real triage

Real crews prioritize the fires most likely to spread to more fuel, not just the biggest flame. Try to douse the leading edge instead of chasing whichever tile lit up last.
